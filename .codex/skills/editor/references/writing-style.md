# Writing style

The published corpus is narrative and argumentative rather than academic. Articles commonly:

This is a living guide. Treat the source articles in `content/english/blog/` as authoritative evidence and only promote a new pattern here after it recurs or the user explicitly confirms it as intentional.

- Open with a lived incident, observed leadership problem, or provocative question.
- Move from a person or event to the surrounding system: incentives, culture, process, power, communication, or accountability.
- Use first person honestly, then widen the lesson to leaders and organisations.
- Mix short emphatic sentences with longer reflective paragraphs.
- Use rhetorical questions and direct challenges to the reader.
- Organise practical guidance under principles, phases, levels, or toolkits.
- End with a sharp synthesis, question, or aphoristic line.

Recurring vocabulary includes resilient systems, culture, accountability, psychological safety, proactive work, paper cuts, shared language, business impact, and cost of inaction.

Preserve human roughness where it carries voice, but correct grammar, ambiguity, repetition, and accidental claims. Use British English. Do not imitate every grammatical error in the source corpus.
